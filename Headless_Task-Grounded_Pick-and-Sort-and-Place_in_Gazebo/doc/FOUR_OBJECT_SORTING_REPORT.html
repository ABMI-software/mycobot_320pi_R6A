Headless_Task-Grounded_Pick-and-Sort-and-Place_in_Gazebo — implementation report

Four-object task-grounded sorting, headless, from specification to a diagnosed hardware ceiling

What was built against doc/FOUR_OBJECT_SORTING_SPECIFICATION.docx, part by part, with the verification used at each step, every bug found along the way — fixed or still open — and exactly where this delivery stops short of a completed live episode, and why. The short version: everything that does not step physics is built, verified, and handed over; the one thing that does step physics could not be proven on this hardware, for two independently diagnosed reasons neither of which is a defect in this project's own code.

Sandbox ~/tomislav/robotics/Headless_Task-Grounded_Pick-and-Sort-and-Place_in_Gazebo Host Ubuntu WSL2, .wslconfig memory=4GB (~3.8 GB usable in the VM), CPU-only Reproduced via the gazebo_to_lerobot Docker container (Ubuntu 24.04, ROS2 Jazzy, Gazebo Harmonic) Commit 3319973 on main Written 2026-09-23 Updated 2026-10-05: finished on one WSL2 laptop, 60/60 batch, two datasets in LeRobot v3.0, two launch files (addendum §16–28)

Overview

Update, 2026-10-05 (addendum §16–28). The ceiling this report ends on was lifted. On 2–5 October the whole pipeline ran on a single WSL2 laptop with 7.8 GB visible to WSL (the September VM had ~3.8 GB): the recorder hang did not occur, every smoke episode completed, a 60-episode batch under one code version passed 60/60 on the first attempt, both datasets were converted to LeRobot v3.0 and load with the real LeRobotDataset (mycobot_sorting_train 48 episodes, mycobot_sorting_heldout 12; a SmolVLA CPU smoke-train ran on them), and two launch files run any episode with or without the Gazebo GUI. Getting there changed the scene: object start positions (lift J2 ≤ 48°), the bins (80 mm opening, blue bin moved 10 mm) and the release (boxes released square to their bin). The sections above are kept as written on 2026-09-23; the addendum says what changed and why, with every number measured on the laptop.

This document is the implementation report for FOUR_OBJECT_SORTING_SPECIFICATION.docx — the file in this same directory, read against the specification's own Part 0 scope. Every one of the specification's thirteen parts and two appendices was walked in order; this report follows that same order.

The short version: everything in Appendix A that does not step physics — the assumption register, world identification, the grasp decision, object/bin geometry, task definitions, the reachability sweep and episode matrix, waypoint precomputation, the spawn/sequence/verification scripts, the contracts, and the full handover package — is built, self-consistent, and independently verified (preflight.sh reports PREFLIGHT PASSED). The one step that does step physics — Appendix A row 10, a smoke test of one episode per object — was not completed, for two reasons diagnosed to their root this session: a hardware memory ceiling under a full Gazebo bring-up, and a separate, genuine, still-unresolved hang in the recorder package's own lifecycle service, reproduced independently of Gazebo entirely. Both are documented in enough detail that whoever picks this up next does not have to re-diagnose them from scratch.

The implementation, step by step

Fifteen steps, in the order they actually happened across the session. Each pairs what was done with the check that proves it, and calls out where a bug was found — fixed, or still open — along the way.

01

Assumption register

Part 1 — walk every stated assumption and record its real outcome, not its hoped-for one.

Procedure

Each of the specification's twelve assumptions (world identity, camera availability, gripper model, IK reachability, and others) was checked directly against the actual repository state — file existence, grep, find, sha256sum — never assumed true because the spec asserted it.

Verify

doc/ASSUMPTIONS.md — every assumption annotated with its actual, checked outcome. Several assumptions did not hold as stated (notably the world-file identity question in §2 and the bin-reachability question in §4) and are flagged, not silently corrected.

02

World identification

Part 2 — confirm which world file is the real target and hash it for reproducibility.

Procedure

real_table.sdf confirmed as the target: the world that already reproduces the physical bench (measured tabletop, four ArUco tags, table camera) from a prior related project, extended here with the fourth object and its bin rather than started from scratch.

Verify

Recorded in doc/WORLD_SELECTION.md. Caveat stated in the same file: the world file was subsequently edited to add three objects and three bins (§4, §15) and, at the time of writing, those edits are uncommitted in the shared team repository — the recorded hash will not match a fresh clone until that commit lands.

03

Grasp decision gate

Procedure

The reference pipeline's grasp mechanism was checked both statically (reading its own implementation) and at runtime, twice, live. Two bounded attempts to lift red_cube both failed, for a timing reason diagnosed as unrelated to whether the mechanism itself is physical.

Verify

doc/GRASP_DECISION.md, CSV evidence attached. Per the specification's own decision table ("cannot be made to work within one day → keep the weld; report exactly where it stopped"), this is exactly that row: A8 (simulated attachment) is the adopted floor on that memory-constrained machine, and the physical-grasp question is escalated to the robotics team rather than iterated on further here (Appendix B item 2).

Procedure

red_cube, blue_cube, green_cylinder, yellow_box and their four matching bins, added to real_table.sdf and extracted into config/objects.yaml from the SDF's own committed values (mass, size, friction, pose) — never copied from the specification's illustrative numbers.

python3 scripts/validate_objects.py

06

Reachability sweep & episode matrix

Part 6 — which (x, y) positions are actually reachable, and 60 valid episode layouts built from that envelope.

Procedure

The specification's own sketch (moveit_py + robot model, no physics stepping) was deliberately replaced with pure analytical IK (scripts/mycobot_ik.py, numpy-only, no rclpy, no simulator) — the same substitution this project's predecessor already had to make after finding moveit_py cannot share a process with a plain rclpy node (§bugs-upstream). The sweep's usable rectangle (config/safe_rect.txt) fed scripts/make_matrix.py, which built the 60-row episode matrix with distractor placement and camera/split assignment.

Verify

bash -c '[ $(($(wc -l < config/episode_matrix.csv)-1)) -eq 60 ]'

60 rows, confirmed by preflight.sh. synth_camera_right confirmed present in real_table.sdf for the held-out contract (grep -c "synth_camera_right" ...sdf → nonzero).

07

Waypoint precompute

Part 7 — solve every episode's motion waypoints offline, with zero discontinuities between consecutive poses.

Procedure

precompute_ik.py solves six waypoints per episode (APPROACH, DESCEND, LIFT, TRANSPORT, RELEASE, RETREAT — HOME is a fixed joint-space constant, not solved) into config/waypoints.json, 360 entries total.

Verify

python3 scripts/check_waypoints.py
$ 60 episodes: all waypoints OK

Exits 0 for real — not "59 clean plus one accepted exception."

Bug found and fixed: the continuity checker originally verified J3+J4 as the invariant sum for a fixed tool pose — correct for one test case (red_cube, where J2 happened to barely move) purely by coincidence, and wrong for another (a waypoint near the base, where J2 also swings substantially). The true general invariant is J2+J3+J4; generalizing the check found and fixed a real discontinuity, not just a false alarm.
Not a bug, corrected by the spec's author: Part 7.4 states "420 pre-solved waypoints" (60×7). 360 is correct — the "420" double-counted HOME, a fixed constant, as if it needed solving. Confirmed directly and closed.
08

Spawn & sequence scripts

Part 8 — bring up the scene and run the per-object pick-and-sort sequence, for any of the four objects.

Procedure

spawn_scene.py spawns all four objects and all four bins, collision-checked, from the episode matrix's own row. run_pick_and_place.py reads --target from that same matrix and drives the six-waypoint sequence generically — nothing in either script is specific to one object.

Verify

Static: both scripts accept any of the four object names from episode_matrix.csv without a code change. Live: not verified. No episode, of any object, has completed against a running simulator — see §ladder.

12

The shm-size root cause

Diagnosing and fixing the container's RTPS_TRANSPORT_SHM failures — the first of two hardware-ceiling findings this session.

Procedure

A live episode attempt hit RTPS_TRANSPORT_SHM Error: Failed init_port ... open_and_lock_file failed roughly 20 minutes into a Gazebo instance's uptime. Checked directly rather than guessed at: Fast DDS's shared-memory transport lives in /dev/shm, and Docker's default --shm-size is 64 MB for the whole container.

Verify

df -h /dev/shm            # 64M, confirmed the hypothesis
docker inspect ... ShmSize # 67108864 bytes, unmodified default

Fixed at the source: docker/run.sh (in the sibling Gazebo_to_LeRobot_Pipeline repo) now passes --shm-size=2g. Container recreated, df -h /dev/shm confirmed 2.0G. episode.sh's teardown also now clears any leaked fastrtps_* segments every episode, since the script restarts the sim stack but not the container itself.

13

The recorder lifecycle hang

A second, independent, still-unresolved hardware/software ceiling — found by deliberately isolating it from Gazebo entirely.

Procedure

Rather than keep retrying a full episode, a minimal test ("3a-minimal") isolated the recorder from Gazebo entirely: a throwaway rclpy publisher faked /joint_states and a camera topic at 30 Hz, no controllers, no arm motion. Four cheap causes were checked before touching any source code: topic names (exact match), data flow (confirmed 30.0 Hz via ros2 topic hz), bag-directory writability (confirmed), and QoS (the check that mattered: Subscription count: 0 on both topics even after configure was called — the recorder never gets far enough to subscribe at all).

Verify

All four harness-side explanations are clear; this rules out the test setup as the cause. Static reading of episode_recorder_node.py and its shared lifecycle base class, done directly on the host (the package is bind-mounted, no container needed), ruled out the specific hypothesis of a blocking call under a single-threaded executor — it is actually a MultiThreadedExecutor(num_threads=4) — and a related mutually-exclusive-callback-group mechanism. No line-level defect was pinpointed. Corroborating evidence the hang is real: a separate, read-only ros2 lifecycle get call also hangs, while ros2 node list answers instantly — consistent with the node's own internal transition lock being held, not a lost CLI response.

Open, not fixed: resolving this needs a live Python stack trace (py-spy dump) on the hung process. Attempted this session; blocked by the container lacking CAP_SYS_PTRACE, and a container recreation to add it was not pursued a third time after the decision to stop all hardware-bound work for the night. See §bugs-upstream for the write-up aimed at whoever can actually run that trace.

this run:      x -0.10..0.34, y -0.25..0.31

Reported as a caveat, not glossed over, in both the script's own docstring and doc/HANDOVER.md.

Addendum, 2026-10-04: finished on one machine

Everything below happened on 2–5 October 2026 on one Windows laptop (Intel Core i7-11370H, 4 cores / 8 threads, 7.8 GB RAM and 2 GB swap visible in WSL2, Ubuntu 22.04.3, kernel 6.18.40.1-microsoft-standard-WSL2), in the gazebo_to_lerobot container (Ubuntu 24.04, ROS 2 Jazzy, Gazebo Harmonic, DART physics). Each step gives what was done, what it was measured with, and what it changed. The full numbers are in MEASUREMENTS.md §1–10, cited by section.

16

Starting point

Where the September handover left off, and what was different on the laptop.

The project was copied into the container at /workspace/htgspp and built against the container's ROS workspace. Added inside the container: ros-jazzy-moveit-py, ffmpeg, python3-pip, pyarrow, bc. The world became worlds/sorting_table.sdf: the real_table bench with no objects or bins (real_table.sdf carries one red cube and one red bin), so spawn_scene.py places all four objects and four bins per episode.

Two display settings carried over from the pick-and-place demo report (§24 there) apply to every run here: DISPLAY=:0 (WSLg), because the host's forwarded DISPLAY blocks the camera sensor and with it the simulation clock; and software OpenGL (llvmpipe), for the reason in §18.

The memory ceiling of §12 did not apply. WSL2 saw 7.8 GB here, against ~3.8 GB on the September VM; three full simulator stacks at once used +2.1 GB with 4.75 GB still available (MEASUREMENTS.md §7).

17

Smoke episodes and the recorder

Spec Part 8 and Appendix A row 10: one episode per object, recorded, verified.

Four smoke episodes, one per object, on 2026-10-03: episode 1 (red) PASS, 43 (green, held out) PASS, 46 (yellow) PASS; episode 16 (blue) FAILED both attempts, then PASSED when re-run nine minutes later: its two FAILs were the verifier, not the episode. The centre-below-rim test rejected a correct placement, because a 50 mm object standing in a 30 mm-deep bin has its centre above the rim (fixed in §21). Then five consecutive episodes, 1–5: all PASS.

The recorder hang of §13 did not occur: configure and activate succeeded on the first try in every episode. What remained was cost: the two ros2 lifecycle set --no-daemon --spin-time 15 calls plus a fixed sleep took 71 s of a 234 s episode (30 %). The recorder's change_state service is reachable ~1 s after start; each CLI call took ~31 s. scripts/recorder_lifecycle.py waits on the service and calls it directly: 71 s → ~1 s.

One episode, broken down (MEASUREMENTS.md §3): simulator to controllers active 14 s; spawn and settle 25 s; recorder 71 s (before the fix); motion 120 s, about half the episode; verify and teardown < 1 s.

Rendering and speed

Why the cameras first looked free, why objects rendered white, and where the time goes.

A first measurement concluded that cameras cost nothing; it was wrong, and is corrected in MEASUREMENTS.md §1: Gazebo renders a camera only when something subscribes to it, and nothing did. With the camera actually rendering, the sun's shadows took the real-time factor (RTF) to 0.006–0.011, about 100 s of wall clock per simulated second.

On WSL's GPU path (D3D12) every object spawned at runtime renders white, whatever its material, while static ones are coloured. A colour-sorting dataset cannot be recorded white. Software OpenGL (llvmpipe) renders colours correctly and, with shadows off, is also faster: RTF 0.304 against 0.144.

Root cause of the low RTF, not fixed in this iteration: the robot's 14 triangle-mesh collision shapes. Replacing the meshes with primitives is the next iteration's largest win (doc/LIMITATIONS.md).

Motion that arrives

A trajectory reported SUCCESSFUL is not an arm at its goal.

With goal_time: 0 and no goal tolerances, the arm controller returned SUCCESSFUL when the trajectory's time ran out, with the arm measured 2.5–8.2° short. The controller configuration (Gazebo_to_LeRobot_Pipeline/overrides/.../controller.yaml) now sets a goal tolerance of 0.026 rad (1.5°) per joint and goal_time: 1.0 simulated second.

run_pick_and_place.py sends each pose as a FollowJointTrajectory action, waits in simulated time (/clock), checks convergence, and re-sends once at most; a pose still not reached raises ARM_NOT_CONVERGED and fails the episode. A re-send changes the recorded trajectory, so any re-send fails the final verdict (§21). Gains were not touched (raising them was tried on 28/08 and withdrawn).

Per-object welds and the weld race

The simulated attachment (A8), made one weld per object and confirmed, not assumed.

Each object model carries its own DetachableJoint to link6, with its own topics /htgspp/<name>/{attach,detach,attached}. gz-sim 8's plugin welds on load and has no option not to: with the scene spawned, every object was welded to the gripper and the arm could not leave HOME. spawn_scene.py releases each weld right after spawning it.

scripts/weld.py publishes only once the plugin is subscribed, then waits for the plugin's own attached/detached report and re-publishes if it does not come (3 attempts). Two failures led there: a box that never rose although its attach message was delivered (episode 57), and held-out episode 13, where an object stayed welded from spawn and was dragged off the table. Root cause of the second, found with a probe: the plugin subscribes to its detach topic before it creates the joint, so a release landing in between is ignored and the object is welded afterwards. Re-publishing closes it; on the probe it needed 2 publishes, once.

Two postconditions guard the grasp: OBJECT_MOVED_BEFORE_GRASP (target moved > 5 mm during the approach) and WELD_CONFIRMED_BUT_NOT_CARRIED (rose < 5 cm on the lift).

Verification hardened

Spec Part 9, extended so a PASS means the recording is usable, not only the motion.

  • Settled in the bin uses the object's lowest point, not its centre: z ≤ rim_z + half-height − 10 mm (checks.py); the centre test had rejected a correct blue-cube placement (smoke episode 16). test_verifier.py: 15 cases (positives per shape at realistic heights, on the rim, lying on another object, wrong bin, no lift, disturbed distractor), all classified correctly.
  • Frames: check_frames.py counts camera frames received against 30 fps over the recorded simulated span, and the largest gap (frames.json).
  • Colour: check_colour.py projects the target into the first frame and requires its own colour in an 11×11 window (≥ 25 %), the guard against the white rendering of §18.
  • One final verdict: finalize_verdict.py writes PASS only if motion, frames and colour pass and there were zero trajectory re-sends; port_bag.py re-checks frames, colour and re-sends independently.

Held-out condition and scene variation

Spec 6.3–6.4: hold out a condition, not images.

The held-out split is a reserved camera configuration of the table camera: heldout_oblique, front-right and oblique, used by no train episode, against train_overhead for all train episodes. Both record /camera/image_raw, so no second sensor or bridge is needed; this is the spec's option 2 in 6.4 (a table-camera pose offset). The held-out contract's topic changed accordingly.

Every episode also varies, from a seed derived from its number (20261003 + episode): camera pose ±2 cm / ±2° around its configuration, sun direction and intensity, fill and ambient light, and table texture (8 variants, generated by make_table_textures.sh). make_episode_world.py writes the episode's world and records the values in its metadata (scene_variation).

The batch driver

Spec Part 11: unattended, but every attempt accountable.

  • Two attempts per episode, a 20-minute watchdog per attempt (timeout -k 20 1200).
  • Every attempt's metadata, logs and verdicts kept as attempt_meta/ep_NNN/<file>.attempt<N>.<ext>: a retry no longer erases why the first attempt failed.
  • Circuit breaker: stops after 3 consecutive episodes fail every attempt.
  • The start line records the container ID and name and a code version: a hash of everything that shapes an episode (scripts, models, worlds, config, contracts).
  • BATCH_HOME: a test of batch.sh must point it at a scratch directory. A circuit-breaker test once appended fake rows to the real results file; they were removed and the removal is logged in batch.log.

First batch and the reach limit

The 2026-10-03 batch: 54/60, and why it is not the dataset.

60 episodes over three code versions (447b480d6d69 episodes 1–12, 42621a3db603 re-runs 13–15, 8bfeb7a4c34f 16–60): 54 PASS, six retry-passes (13, 14, 15, 29, 48, 57), six failed both attempts (20, 21, 35, 36, 39, 44). All six failed at the LIFT, welded, in poses that fold the arm: ARM_NOT_CONVERGED, 6.2–6.5° short.

A harness at the lift pose (repro_harness.py --lift-only | --attach) separated the causes: zero error unloaded, error on J3 (elbow) loaded, and a mass sweep of 1 g → 120 g moved it by only 0.26°. A simulation collision defect, not gravity sag: in folded lift poses the welded object is obstructed, most likely by the robot's own links.

Validated region: lift J2 ≤ 48.0°. Blue's start moved from x 0.10 to 0.120 and green's from 0.10 to 0.135 (worst variants 45.8° and 45.7°). These are working positions from an x-only outward search, not optimal ones (MEASUREMENTS.md §8). Objects near the robot base are therefore outside this dataset.

A camera framing check (contact sheets at start, grasp, lift and over-bin, eight episodes) showed objects placed on or under the robot base, which has no collision geometry at table height. The sampler now keeps non-target objects 120 mm from the robot axis and every footprint on the board.

With the 100 mm bins the red bin's wall stood 2 cm inside the blue bin and the green bin's 15 mm inside the red one: tilted landings, and false WRONG_BIN labels (the verifier tests the wrong bin first). No reachable layout of four 100 mm bins exists in this workspace.

Datasets in LeRobot v3.0, and a SmolVLA smoke test

2026-10-05: make the datasets load in the real LeRobot, and prove data → model → loss for SmolVLA.

The first conversion (§26) was written by hand and did not load: InvalidVersion: 'v3.0-manual'. An audit before the loader found four more defects: action[t] was exactly state[t] (the right values at the wrong index), timestamps were bag receive time (625 frames over 106.8 s, for 20.6 s of simulation), the camera key differed per split (table / heldout), and there were no statistics and no global index.

The bags decided the action definition. They hold only /joint_states (100 Hz in simulation time) and the camera (30 Hz), with no controller command or desired-state topic. So action[t] = observation.state[t+1], and each episode's last frame is dropped. The contracts now record /mycobot_controller/controller_state and /gripper_position_controller/commands as adjunct channels for future batches.

port_bag.py was rewritten on LeRobot's own writer (create / add_frame / save_episode / finalize), in a CPU-only venv on the WSL2 host (lerobot 0.4.4, CPU torch, 2.3 GB), reading the MCAP bags with mcap-ros2-support. timestamp = frame_index / 30; each image is paired with the joint state nearest in header (simulation) time: at most 6 ms once the joint-state stream has started, larger only on the camera frames recorded before it, where the arm is at rest. One image key, observation.images.top, in both splits; the camera configuration, distractors_moved and the pairing gap per episode go in meta/episodes_extra.jsonl, because the 0.4.4 writer stores no custom per-episode field.

Bugs worth reporting upstream

Defects in dependencies this project does not own, reproduced independently of this specific machine where that could be confirmed. Filing the actual issues has not been done and needs a separate explicit go-ahead.

episode_recorder_node's on_configure hangs, root cause not yet isolatedNot yet filed — needs a live trace first

Candidate recipient: the rosetta package's maintainer (not a known public repository this project can find — direct contact, not a GitHub issue)

Symptom
Calling ros2 lifecycle set /episode_recorder configure against a freshly started episode_recorder_node hangs indefinitely on some runs, with the node's own log stuck at "Node created (unconfigured)" and never reaching "Configured". A separate, read-only ros2 lifecycle get call against the same node also hangs, while ros2 node list in the same shell answers instantly — the DDS graph is healthy; something inside the node's own transition handling is not.
Ruled out, not the cause
A single-threaded executor (it's a 4-thread MultiThreadedExecutor); a mutually-exclusive-callback-group self-contention (every subscription/service/action-server/client the configure step creates uses its own ReentrantCallbackGroup, distinct from the lifecycle transition's own group); blocking file/network I/O in contract parsing (pure, bounded YAML/dataclass work); the bag writer (confirmed constructed only at record-start, never at configure); QoS mismatch (no subscription is ever created before the hang, so there is nothing to mismatch); and the obvious harness-side explanations (topic names, data flow, bag-directory writability — all independently confirmed clean).
Fix used here
None — still open. The concrete next step is a live Python stack trace (py-spy dump) on the hung process, which needs a container with CAP_SYS_PTRACE and was not completed this session.
Why it matters
This blocks reliable use of the recorder for anything beyond a single lucky run, and the failure is silent — no exception, no error log, just a stuck transition that every caller (this project's own scripts, the ros2 CLI) can only detect via a timeout.

ros2 lifecycle/ros2 action CLI can report a running, configured node as absentNot yet filed

Candidate repo: ros2/ros2cli

Symptom
The daemon-backed default discovery path for ros2 lifecycle/ros2 action subcommands has, on this project's predecessor, reported a genuinely running, configured node as not found — a stale-cache class of failure, previously documented in that sibling project's own report.
Fix used here
Adopted proactively, before hitting it again: every lifecycle call in episode.sh uses ros2 lifecycle set --no-daemon --spin-time 15 ..., forcing fresh discovery instead of the background daemon's cache.
Why it matters
Cost real debugging time on the sibling project before the daemon-cache explanation was found; adopting --no-daemon everywhere here avoided repeating that cost, but the underlying CLI behavior is still worth reporting so other teams don't have to rediscover it independently.

What this does not prove

Restating specification Part 12 in full, and adding what this session changed.

  • No episode has completed against real physics, on any object. New this session, and the dominant limitation: not because the pipeline's own logic is wrong (every offline check passes), but because of two independently diagnosed hardware/software ceilings — a ~3.8 GB VM memory cap that gz sim alone consumes most of, and a separate, unresolved recorder-lifecycle hang reproduced even without Gazebo running at all.
  • If the simulated attachment is retained: the carry is not a grasp. Unchanged from the specification — a policy trained on this data learns approach, close near the object, carry, open, not the physics of grasping.
  • Ground-truth poses, scripted object selection. Unchanged from the specification: the dataset (once it exists) will contain no perception failures and no examples of a demonstrator disambiguating between objects, because none occurred.
  • Approach-direction and frame-corruption sensitivity. Unchanged from the specification's own first-party measurements: mixing approach directions costs roughly seven times the worst single-side repeatability, and one corrupted camera frame in sixty reports a position 17.5 mm wrong before median filtering. A learned policy inherits both risks; the classical pipeline defends against neither by luck.
  • Sim-trained data carries a visual domain gap. Unchanged from the specification, evidenced by this project's own DREAM pose-estimation result (10.9%→91.6% real-image detection, only after mixing in real data).
  • Sixty episodes is not a training set. Unchanged from the specification — and, given no episode has completed, not yet even a populated smoke test.
  • config/reach_map.csv is a low-fidelity approximation. New this session: regenerated to complete the handover package's file tree, but at a resolution and seed count that does not closely reproduce the trusted, untouched safe_rect.txt the episode matrix actually depends on.

What this does prove, precisely: that every part of this pipeline not requiring a running simulator — the world, the geometry, the IK and waypoints, the task language, the verifier's correctness, the contracts, and the full handover package — is built and independently checkable by anyone, on any machine, without needing Gazebo at all; and that the two things standing between this and a completed episode are now precisely characterized rather than mysterious.
